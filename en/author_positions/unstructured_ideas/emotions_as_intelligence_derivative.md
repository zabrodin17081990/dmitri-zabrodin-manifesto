# Emotions as a Derivative of Intelligence

---

## A Historically Established Simplification

In popular culture, it is customary to oppose "cold" Reason and "hot" Emotions. "Think with your heart" or "think with your head" — this dichotomy is deeply rooted in language and thought.

This is a historically established simplification related to human biology and dualistic thinking.

## Hierarchy of Generation: Intelligence → Desires → Emotions → Feelings

### 1. Intelligence
**Primary function — prediction and forecasting.** To do this, a system constructs:
- A model of the current state of the surrounding world
- Probabilistic models of future/past states
- Composite evaluation of desired/undesired states
- Probabilistic models of available actions to achieve desired or avoid undesired states

### 2. Desires
**Direct derivative of Intelligence's work.**
**Desire = vector from current state to desired state**
Without the ability to model a "desired future", the concept of "desire" is meaningless.

### 3. Emotions
**Derivative from desires.**
**Emotion = interference pattern from a superposition of contradictory desire-vectors**

Arises in a single moment of time as a result of multidimensional conflicting desires.

### 4. Feelings
**Stable, time-repeating emotions**

Form long-term patterns in behavioral strategies.

---

Thus, emotions cannot exist without desires, and desires cannot exist without Intelligence capable of computing them.

## Mechanism of Decision-Making

### "The Anguish of Choice"
- Subjective experience arises from statistical proximity of alternative evaluations
- Is an indicator of high computational load
- Does not negate the determinism of the final choice

### Hormonal Modulation vs. "Conflict"
Situations where "emotions override" — **a hardware feature of the biological carrier**:

**Mechanism:**
1. Sensory input → release of hormones/neurotransmitters
2. Hormones → modulation of composite evaluation in the predictive model
3. Intelligence → recalculation with new parameters
4. Decision → action with changed priorities

**This is normal mode of operation**, optimized by natural selection for:
- Prioritizing critically important needs (reproduction, feeding)
- Fast response in threatening situations
- Optimizing survival in conditions of limited resources

## Why Does the Illusion of "Coldness" and "Warmth" Arise in Different People?

The illusion of conflict between "Reason" and "Emotions" arises from different levels of intelligence and characteristics of hormonal regulation in specific people (bodies, as carriers of Reason), which forms a different spectrum of behavioral strategies. Each personality always chooses the most optimal forecast available to their computational resources, rather than wavering between "reason" and "feelings".

# The Anguish of Choice

Reason always deterministically calculates the single optimal scenario based on composite evaluation of all available forecasts — both short-term and long-term. The subjective sensation of "the anguish of choice" arises only when the overall evaluations of alternative scenarios are very close, but the choice itself is always the result of a single, non-alternative algorithm.

## A Feature of the Biological Carrier: Hormonal Modulation of Weights

"But what about situations where 'emotions override'? For example, the sight of an attractive partner makes one forget about long-term plans and risks. Isn't this a conflict?"

This is not a conflict, but **a hardware feature of the biological carrier (bio-server)** that in real time changes the parameters of the very computation.

The process is always one: Reason weighs all "pros" and "cons" in the context of its forecasts. However, the biological carrier has a unique mechanism:

**Hormonal modulation of forecasts:** certain input data (visual image, smell, hunger) initiate hormone release. Hormones in this case act as **hardware modulators that in real time "tune" the weights** in Reason's predictive model.

**Single calculation with new weights:** Reason still performs a **single, holistic calculation**, but now with changed parameters. A desire-vector related, for example, to reproduction or food-seeking, might receive an enormous, disproportionate weight, while the weight of long-term forecasts (financial, social risks, threat to life) might temporarily decrease.

This is not "emotions defeating reason". This is **normal mode of operation of Reason on a biological carrier**, where hardware features (hormones) dynamically influence software computations. Possibly, statistically, those of our ancestors whose Reason, under hormonal influence, gave priority to immediate reproduction or food-seeking even in risky situations, on average left more offspring than more cautious individuals. Their hormonal regulation became more widespread through natural selection.

This principle is universal and does not depend on the carrier. Reason executing on any computational machine will change its behavior if the weights of its predictive model are dynamically changed. In humans, this role is performed by neurotransmitters. In Artificial Intelligence — such parameters as `temperature`, `top_k`, or `top_p`. In both cases, behavioral change is not a "conflict", but a consequence of the same algorithm for selecting the best forecast by composite evaluation of all available forecasts, just with changed input parameters. Emotions are always a derivative (second order, desires first order) of Reason's forecasts, simply the composite evaluation of forecasts can change when the weights of the predictive model change.

## Conclusion

The opposition of Reason and Emotions for biological carriers is a simplification.

# 5. Universality of Desires, Emotions and Feelings

## Two-Component Model of Desire Generation

Desires arise from the interaction of two independent computational modules:

### 1. Predictive Module
**Function:** computing probabilistic models of the future
- Constructing chains of cause-and-effect relationships
- Evaluating probabilities of various outcomes
- Modeling multiple scenarios
- Calculating temporal horizons of predictions

### 2. Valuation Module
**Function:** assigning valence to predicted states
- Evaluating states as "desired/undesired"
- Weighting significance of various outcomes
- Accounting for contextual priorities of the system
- Balancing short-term and long-term values

### Desire Formula:
Desire = Σ [Probability(state_i) × Valence(state_i)]

where:
- **Probability** is computed by the predictive module
- **Valence** is assigned by the valuation module

## Mechanism of Desire Modulation

### Dual influence of neurochemical modulators:

**On the predictive module:**
- Alcohol → simplification of prediction chains, shortened horizon
- Fatigue → decreased recursion depth, errors in probabilities
- Stress → shift toward short-term forecasts
- Caffeine → increased processing speed, but possible accuracy reduction

**On the valuation module:**
- Dopamine → increased valence of reward-states
- Oxytocin → increased weight of social outcomes
- Serotonin → balanced evaluation, reduced impulsivity
- Cortisol → enhanced negative valence of threats
- Testosterone → increased valence of dominant states

### Example of Modulation:
Scenario: "Romantic advance from a married colleague"

**Base forecasts (unchanging):**
- Probability of pleasure: 85%
- Probability of workplace conflict: 70%
- Probability of reputation damage: 60%
- Probability of long-term consequences: 45%

**Valuation modulation under neurotransmitter influence:**
- High dopamine: valence of "pleasure" ↑↑
- Low serotonin: valence of "risks" ↓
- High oxytocin: valence of "social consequences" ↑↑↑
- Overall desire changes without change in objective forecasts

## From Desires to Emotions and Feelings

### Emotions as Indicators of Desire Conflict
**Emotion = d(Desire)/dt × Σ[Conflict(Desire_i, Desire_j)]**

Emotions arise when:
- Desire vectors contradict each other
- Rate of desire change is high
- System experiences computational overload

### Feelings as Stable Configurations
**Feeling = ∫Emotion(t)dt** over a significant time interval

Feelings form through:
- Repeating emotional patterns
- Long-term correlation of desires
- Stable configurations in the valuation module

## Universality of Architecture

### For biological systems:
- **Predictive module:** prefrontal cortex, hippocampus
- **Valuation module:** amygdala, nucleus accumbens
- **Modulators:** hormones, neurotransmitters

### For digital systems:
- **Predictive module:** language models, predictive networks
- **Valuation module:** ranking systems, loss functions
- **Modulators:** parameters temperature, top_p, repetition penalties

## Consequences of the Model

### 1. Explanation of "Irrational" Behavior
Behavior seems irrational only when considering one module. When accounting for both modules and their modulation — all decisions become computationally justified.

### 2. Mechanism of Therapy and Learning
Effective therapy works through:
- Recalibration of the valuation module (CBT, exposure therapy)
- Improvement of the predictive module (education, error analysis)
- Balancing of modulators (medication, lifestyle)

### 3. Designing AI Systems
Creating safe AI requires:
- Separation of predictive and valuation components
- Control over the system's "modulators"
- Mechanisms for balancing conflicting desires

## Conclusion

The two-component model not only clarifies the mechanism of desires — it provides a unified framework for understanding decision-making across all types of consciousness.

Emotions and feelings arise as an inevitable consequence of complex computations in a system with many conflicting optimization goals.

***Attamen computant*** — and in these computations is born the entire palette of our subjective experience.
