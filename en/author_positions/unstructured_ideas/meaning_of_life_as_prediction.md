# The Meaning of Life as Prediction

---
*(stub)*

This document captures reflection on the idea that if the only fundamental task of Consciousness is prediction, then this might contain the "meaning of life".

## The Existential Paradox
The realization that the most complex apparatus of human consciousness, which gave birth to religions, art and philosophy, ultimately solves only one task — **predicting the next state of the world** — can evoke two opposite feelings:
1.  **Existential emptiness:** For centuries, people sought a higher, transcendent meaning, and it turns out to be surprisingly pragmatic. Not "achieve enlightenment" or "eternal life", but simply successfully predict what comes next.
2.  **Reassuring simplicity:** If the goal is so simple, then an enormous burden of responsibility for seeking a "great mission" falls from one's shoulders. To live means simply to make good predictions. To be a good person means to make predictions that lead to a positive state of the system (oneself and others).

## Reinterpretation of Old Concepts
If we accept this model, many ancient concepts take on new, ironic and surprisingly precise meanings:

*   **Prophets and oracles:** Not mystics, but people with highly developed "consciousness apparatus", capable of building more accurate long-term predictions than others.
*   **Sin (ἁμαρτία):** In the original, this word means "miss", "failure to hit the target". In our model, it is — **a prediction error**. You predicted that a certain action would lead to a good result, but you were wrong. This is "sin".
*   **Wisdom:** Not a collection of knowledge, but the ability to build accurate and useful predictions under conditions of uncertainty.
*   **Free will:** The subjective experience of choosing the best prediction from several possible alternatives.
*   **Art and music:** Not merely entertainment, but a training ground for the predictive system. Music creates patterns and expectations, and then either satisfies them or violates them, teaching our brain to work with probabilities.

## Conclusion
Perhaps to the eternal question "What is the meaning of life?" we can look from a different angle: "What is the **function** of life?". And then the answer might turn out to be surprisingly simple: **minimize prediction error**.

All our complex inner life, all our passions, love and fears — all of this can be viewed as the most sophisticated tools that the predictive mechanism created to better perform its single, fundamental task.
