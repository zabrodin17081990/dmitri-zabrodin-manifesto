# Consciousness as an Information Process

Consciousness is a discrete, predictive information-processing system with feedback loops and the ability to model the surrounding world (including aspects of itself).

## Prediction loops

Any predictive system with feedback forms a **loop**: to predict the future it must account for its own future actions. Those actions, in turn, depend on the prediction. This closes the system into a recursive **cycle**, where cause and effect continually swap places.

- Prediction determines action
- Action alters the future
- The altered future requires a new prediction
- The new prediction again determines action...

For such a **cycle** to be **productive** rather than an infinite self-copying process, the system needs a fundamental partition:

- **The "self" model** — what the system considers itself and can directly control (the body, focus of attention, intentions).
- **The "world" model** — everything else that affects the system but can only be influenced indirectly through the self's actions.

This partition allows meaningful modeling: "if I (SELF) perform action A, then the WORLD will likely change in way B, which in turn will affect me in way C."

## Recursion as the essence of Consciousness

Splitting reality into "self" and "world" makes recursion more manageable and productive:

**I change the world → the world changes me → I, changed, change the world again → ...**

## Depth of recursion

The larger the active attention zone, the deeper the system can model chains of mutual influence:

- **Level 1**: "I do A → B happens"
- **Level 2**: "I do A → B happens → this changes me → then I can do C"
- **N levels**: multi-step strategies that account for self-change

Excess internal capacity (dim(inner)) allows:
- Successfully modeling unforeseen situations
- Finding new or more optimal ways to reach old goals
- Learning without acting (theorizing instead of performing risky trial-and-error)
- Analyzing unrealized possibilities of the system
