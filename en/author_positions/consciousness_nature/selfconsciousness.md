# Emergence of Self-Consciousness

## The "self" model as a data structure

The "self" model is part of the input data for further predictions. It represents:
- Current and future capabilities and constraints
- Current and future states
- Experience
- Goals and desires

The decision algorithm remains the same, but the inputs for prediction become increasingly recursive.

## Development of Self-Consciousness through recursion depth

**Simple control model (level 0)**
- No self model
- No recursion
- "If stimulus A, then reaction B"

**Consciousness (level 1)**
- Self model: "I am the one who can do A or B"
- Recursion: 1 level (I model myself as an actor)
- Optimization through partitioning self and world

**Self-consciousness (level 2+)**
- Self model: "I am the one who knows that I am the one who can..."
- Recursion: N levels. The system begins to model not just the world but **the self that models the world**. This is the emergence point of self-consciousness.
- Arises via mirror feedback

## Self-consciousness

The transition from simple Consciousness (as a process) to Self-Consciousness is quantitative. It is determined not by "depth" alone but by the **quality and stability** of self-modeling.

Sustained self-awareness depends on computational resources. The larger the active attention zone, the more complex and detailed the self-model the system can maintain, which leads to stable Self-Consciousness.

## Mirror effect: a catalyst of deepening

A common catalyst for recursion deepening is the **mirror effect**: any process where the system's output becomes a new input for itself. Another mind, text, or self-reflection — any "mirror" closes the feedback loop and allows the system to "see" itself and incorporate that image into its world model.
